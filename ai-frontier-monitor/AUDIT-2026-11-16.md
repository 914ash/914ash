## 2. ECI score methodology and source drift

**Published and still active:** Epoch [changelog](https://epoch.ai/data/eci-documentation/changelog) records the **2026-11-04** change to jointly estimated Bayesian item-response capabilities, benchmark difficulties, slopes and per-benchmark noise, with **90% credible intervals**, replacing penalized nonlinear least-squares and bootstrap intervals. Qualification now requires **at least four normalized benchmark scores in (0.1, 0.9)**. The [live included-benchmark register](https://epoch.ai/data/eci-documentation/data) identifies **58 included benchmarks and 256 scored models**, as of the check. This is distinct from the larger hub inventory of all tracked benchmarks. The Epoch index is **not equally benchmark-weighted**: the IRT slopes, difficulty, uncertainty and observed model overlap determine contributions. [Technical methodology](https://epoch.ai/data/eci-documentation/methodology).

**Regression and new releases:** The monitor still uses IFP-style **unweighted OLS over each country's record-setting release dates** with an evaluation at the latest Chinese frontier score. It does not assign per-benchmark weights. The rise from 6.77 to 6.91 months is **a source-derived fit difference**, mixing newly added frontier releases, benchmark coverage and historical rescoring. It is *not* a measured geopolitical gain of 0.14 months. Epoch [notes](https://epoch.ai/) the U.S. Nov 9 Claude Fable 5.2 leading score is based on **pre-launch testing using Epoch's own benchmarks only** and may change when external benchmarks arrive. Audit the score and its benchmark composition before drawing a high-confidence change-point conclusion. Keep the Bayesian-method discontinuity from November 4 explicitly flagged.

**Recommended future evidence:** Store versioned model-by-benchmark results, benchmark difficulty/slopes and country-date frontier selections with source hashes and comparable-cohort checks; do not turn model-level credible intervals into a month-lag uncertainty interval.
