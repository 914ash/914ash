## 2. Most material new evidence — BIS rule effective November 19

**Source:** [BIS interim final rule, 91 FR 74970 / Federal Register document 2026-23529](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-11-19/html/2026-23529.htm), **published and effective November 19, 2026**; see also [BIS implementation/reporting page](https://www.bis.gov/si/exclusionnotesubmission).

- **Overseas compute:** Under new EAR §744.24, covered advanced chips require authorization for certain exports, reexports and in-country transfers when the exporter has knowledge of data-center end use providing access to **more than 12,000,000 aggregate TPP** at one or *linked* data centers for companies headquartered / ultimate-parent headquartered in D:5 countries or Macau. Third-country intermediaries acting on behalf of such firms and downstream changes of end use are expressly addressed. The threshold is an **aggregate total-processing-performance regulatory unit**, **not** 12 million GPU chips, H100e, tokens, or GPU hours. Not every online cloud service becomes prohibited; scope, end-use knowledge, license approval, exceptions and legacy use matter.
- **Transshipment / smuggling exposure:** New advanced-chip licensing also applies to specified transfers to **Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil**, with an exception for certain U.S.-headquartered / U.S.-parent customer cases and reporting. This is **new effective law**, not the earlier Nov 3 proposed supplier contract or an unimplemented congressional allegation. It can affect future foreign cluster access and diversion risk but supplies **no actual Chinese-access reduction number**.
- **Foundry / OSAT due diligence:** Changes close an advanced-node overseas-fabrication screening pathway for certain chip designers, requiring stronger licensing and ownership/end-use checks; [Reuters Nov 19](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/tsmc-tightens-china-ai-chip-curbs-new-us-rules-close-manufacturing-loophole-2026-11-19/) reported TSMC suspending affected **16 nm-and-smaller** Chinese customer orders absent licenses/exceptions. TSMC's compliance is not a measured delta to the Chinese domestic fab/HBM stock. Distinguish the policy mechanism from currently delivered accelerators.
- The rule also **formally rescinds prior AI Diffusion model-weight controls**, which is distinct from Epoch ECI statistical benchmark weights and does not remove the new chip/end-use controls.

**Implication:** IFP's October forecast did **not** include proof of this rule's operational effectiveness. Remote access and some future supply scenarios have newly enacted regulatory downside; no verified count of customer-specific leases terminated or chips stopped supports changing the 2027 modeled **remote 2.834351 million H100e** or any other pathway value.

## 3. Licensed export approvals, actual sales, and company guidance are not the same

- [NVIDIA fiscal 2027 Q3 results](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Third-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx), **published Nov 17, quarter ended Oct 25, 2026**, report **$115.8bn total quarterly revenue; $107.6bn Data Center**; its Q4 guide **assumes no China Data Center compute revenue**. NVIDIA's Nov 17 earnings-call remarks, independently reported by [Reuters on Nov 18](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/nvidia-stops-shipping-us-approved-ai-chips-china-2026-11-18/), specified **$2.4bn licensed Hopper-generation data-center products sold to China** in Q3 and **no longer shipping licensed Hopper to China** at that point. Neither the dollar total nor zero *future guidance* measures installed H100e, and the shipment suspension was not stated to be permanent.
- The [U.S. House Foreign Affairs Committee disclosure Nov 19](https://foreignaffairs.house.gov/news/press-releases/chairman-brian-mast-exercises-legislative-authority-to-publicly-release-ai-chip-license-information-for-china) reports applications seeking **3.4 million NVIDIA/AMD AI chips** and **more than 879,000 approved chip units** in the Dec 2025–May 2026 licensing period. **Approval ≠ purchase ≠ shipment ≠ entry into mainland China ≠ usable training GPU-hours.** Different device types' per-unit FP8 H100-equivalent weights differ. The counts can overlap previously reported sales/arrival stock. Follow-on [Reuters Nov 19](https://www.reuters.com/world/license-information-ai-chips-sent-china-released-by-us-house-committee-2026-11-19/) describes the application records and that deliveries remain unknown.
- Distinguish NVIDIA financial disclosures segmented by **direct customer headquarters** (China/Hong Kong), ultimate recipient, device location and real workloads: a China-headquartered customer's revenue cannot by itself quantify where chips or rented capacity are used, and licenses/sales are not all delivered in the reported period.
- **Assessment:** More evidence of licensed sales in 2026 alongside a current suspension and new stricter rule. These are material contradictions to reconcile prospectively, *not* sufficient reason to overwrite IFP's **legal year-end 2026 .846m or projected 2027 1.346m H100e**.

## 4. Benchmark construction, algorithmic efficiency, and distillation

- [Epoch's 2026-11-04 methodological changelog](https://epoch.ai/data/eci-documentation/changelog) remains the latest verified change: **Bayesian joint fitting of model ability, benchmark difficulty/discrimination and noise** instead of ridge-regularized nonlinear least squares; **90% model-score credible intervals**; model inclusion now requires four scores normalized between .1 and .9. [Included benchmark register](https://epoch.ai/data/eci-documentation/data) on Nov 23: **58 benchmarks; 258 scored models**. There are **no fixed equal benchmark weights**. Separately, the monitor's **one-vote-per-record-setting-release-date OLS** remains an intentionally distinct IFP-style transformation. Benchmark score revisions make the lag nonstationary. No source-supported retrospective adjustment applied.
- **Top-five frontier lab training compute:** IFP's **16× U.S.:China physical training compute** remains a dated **unpublished Pilz estimate**, not an observed ratio derivable from national chip shipment totals or mega-data-center inventories. Public data do not provide a reproducible lab-by-lab, training-vs-inference, period-matched training FLOP panel. No defensible replacement found.
- **Algorithmic efficiency:** The IFP equality of capability per unit of *comparable training compute*, including a similar pretraining/post-training mix, remains a **counterfactual simplification**. National performance gaps, isolated algorithmic innovations, or model-source provenance do not identify a matched causal U.S.–China efficiency coefficient.
- **Distillation benefit and blocking:** The original **S=50% share of physical compute-derived gap recaptured** remains **speculative and not live**; IFP draws from an executive comparison and one controlled study, not a measured China-wide causal share. In a **Nov 20 first-party OpenAI technical disclosure** ([Combatting adversarial distillation](https://openai.com/index/combatting-adversarial-distillation/)), an internal adaptive black-box benchmark reports reduction in successful *protected reasoning-trace extraction* from **54.0% to 0.2%** for previous-request attacks, and **22.9% to 0.9%** for current-request attacks, comparing GPT-6 Astra with original defenses to GPT-6.2 Astra with improved protections. This is an **internal red-team result for specified attacks and products**. It is **not** a finding that 99% of Chinese distillation data was blocked, nor an estimate of changes to S, and the changed defender products complicate causal attribution. Keep the policy slider hypothetical and S unchanged; archive evaluation denominator and attack scope.
- The [Epoch AI chip-arrivals changelog](https://epoch.ai/data/ai-chip-imports-documentation/changelog) still last records **Nov 12 H20 shipment-timing** retrospective revision (+69,000 China H100e cumulative through **June 2026**); do not merge it with prospective year-end China access or top-five training budgets.
