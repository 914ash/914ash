Reproducible model specification

The observational regression and structural causal scenario are distinct estimators.

Observed capability lag

lag = (E − bCN) / mCN − (E − bUS) / mUS

For each developer country, use every release date that broke the previous regional ECI record since January 1, 2024. On a tied date, use the highest score. Regress ECI on months since January 2024 using equal weights, one per record-setting day. Set E to China's last frontier ECI. The resulting horizontal difference is in months.

ECI benchmark weights themselves are fitted by Epoch based on benchmark difficulty and discrimination. This monitoring system does not prescribe them. ECI model-score intervals are not an interval for the estimated national lag.

IFP policy model

L = (1 − S) × K × log₁₀(R)

R: U.S./China physical training-compute ratio (16× assumption). S: share of compute-generated gap recovered by distillation (50% assumption). K: 13 / log₁₀(16) ≈ 10.8 months gained per 10× without distillation. With these assumptions, L = 6.5 months.

Reducing distillation-harvested data by fraction c gives S′ = max(0, S × (1 − 0.25 log₁₀(1/(1−c)))). This elasticity is a modeled relationship rather than a measured causal effect.

Limitations and ramifications: The model neglects safety, military deployment, bio/cyber specialization, talent and industrial dependencies. It is sensitive to benchmark contamination, model release timing, compute conversion and attribution of overseas leases. Controls may impose costs on suppliers and allied cloud providers, provoke adaptation, and alter incentives for safety and security. Neither the monthly lag nor the policy effect is a validated national-security advantage metric. Read IFP's appendices before drawing causal policy conclusions.
